Speed read long emails, bring your own themes, and get around the app with the arrow keys on the Mac too.

**Speed reader**
- Long, plain emails like letters or forwarded articles get a **Speed read** button in the reading toolbar (`Shift+R`). The email plays one word at a time, with each word's focus letter coloured and held on a centre line.
- The button only appears on emails that are mostly prose, so newsletters, receipts and notifications don't get it. Quoted replies and signatures are skipped, so a reply plays only what's new.
- Play, pause, step word by word or drag the progress bar. The speed card has a slider, `−`/`+` buttons and named presets from 200 (Beginner) to 500 (Expert). `↑`/`↓` also change the speed.
- **T** switches the word size, and the options button turns pauses at punctuation on or off. Pheeme remembers each choice.
- The same settings, plus a switch that hides the button, are in **Settings › Appearance › Speed reader**.

**Themes**
- **Settings › Appearance** has **Light theme** and **Dark theme** pickers and an **Import…** button.
- You can import a VS Code colour theme. Colours it leaves out are filled in, and text is adjusted until it's readable on every surface.
- You can also install a Pheeme theme file. Pick several files at once to set both modes from one extension.
- The chosen theme's background is drawn before the app loads, so there's no flash.

**Keyboard**
- `←` moves from an open email to the conversation list, then to the sidebar. `↑`/`↓` step through mailboxes and open each one, and `→` goes back the other way.
- On macOS, `←` works after clicking into an HTML email. Before, the email kept focus. `Cmd+C` still copies text you select in the email.
- A focus ring shows which pane you're in when you move between them with the keyboard.

**Labels**
- In **New label**, the colour grid now spans the whole dialog, and a preview shows the label (with its full nested name) in the colour you picked.
